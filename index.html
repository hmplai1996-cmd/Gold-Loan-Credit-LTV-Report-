How the rate is worked out, and calibration

Daily international gold closes (xaus.com) × the rupee–dollar rate (European Central Bank, via frankfurter.dev) ÷ 31.1035 grams, plus the import duty in force that day and a small market premium. The page fetches new prices when it opens and every 6 hours. These are reference estimates: for LTV, RBI asks for the lower of the 30-day average and the previous day’s close published by IBJA or a SEBI-regulated exchange, so enter the IBJA rate below to calibrate.

    How the numbers are worked out

    • Loans: active gold loans (status S04) with an amount owed above zero. Closed loans are left out.
    • Privacy: on this public page, customer names are shortened to an initial pattern and loan account numbers are partly hidden. Mobile numbers, addresses and ID numbers are not included.
    • Pure-gold weight: each item’s net weight × karat ÷ 24. 10 g of 22K counts as 9.17 g of pure gold; 10 g of 19K as 7.92 g.
    • Gold value at loan: net weight × the open-market rate the valuer recorded that day. The 24K rate at loan is that value ÷ pure-gold weight.
    • Gold value today: each item’s net weight × today’s rate for its karat. By default a karat’s rate is the 24K rate × karat ÷ 24 (22K = 91.7%, 18K = 75%, as jewellers quote); you can type your own rate for 18K to 23K.
    • Amount owed: POS from the report. After a POS & interest upload, POS + interest (or POS alone if you say it already includes interest).
    • LTV: at loan = amount lent ÷ gold value at loan. Today = amount owed now ÷ gold value today.
    • Zones: Red 90% or more, Amber 75% to 90%, Green below 75%. Underwater = 100% or more. These are review bands for this dashboard, not a regulatory class.
    • RBI caps: RBI (Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral) Directions, 2025. Cap by the borrower’s total amount lent: up to ₹2.5 lakh 85%, ₹2.5–5 lakh 80%, above ₹5 lakh 75%. For bullet loans LTV = amount repayable at maturity ÷ gold value. RBI values gold at the lower of the 30-day average and the previous day’s close (IBJA or a SEBI-regulated exchange); enter that rate before acting. Borrowers above ₹2.5 lakh need a detailed credit assessment.
    • Borrower: loans are grouped by Customer ID, else PAN, else name and date of birth. The key is scrambled, so no ID is shown. After a “merge” upload, borrower totals are exact again once the full report is uploaded with “Replace”.
    • Stress test: gold 5%, 10%, 15% and 20% below the rate in use. Loss = amount owed − (gold value × 0.9 − ₹1,000), never below zero per loan.
    • Top-up to 75%: amount owed − 75% of gold value today, for loans above 75%.
    • AI expert: the action plan uses simple rules on the figures shown. Where available, Claude can write a fuller plan from the same figures; customer names are not sent.
    • Gold rate: pick any date in the last year with the calendar, the “Last 1 year” list or the quick buttons (today, 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year ago). Rates are Indian 24K estimates built from the international price, the rupee rate and the import duty, calibrated to the Goodreturns rate of ₹12,458 on 25 Nov 2026 (see the Gold rate tab). Enter the day’s IBJA rate before acting.
    • Exports: CSV, Excel and PDF save the loans the customer list is showing, with every filter and the search applied.
    • Follow-up: contact customers through the standard process (top-up or part-payment requests, reminders). Auction only under the RBI gold-loan directions and board-approved policy.
